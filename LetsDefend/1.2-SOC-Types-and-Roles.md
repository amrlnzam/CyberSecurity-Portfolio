# 🛡️ 1.2 SOC Types and Roles

In this lesson, I explored the definition of a SOC, various operational models, and the key roles that make up a security team.

### **What is a SOC?**
A Security Operation Center (SOC) is a facility where the information security team continuously monitors and analyzes an organization's security posture. The primary goal is to **Detect**, **Analyze**, and **Respond** to cybersecurity incidents using people, processes, and technology.

---

### **SOC Models**
Organizations choose a SOC model based on their security needs and budget:
* **In-house SOC:** A dedicated internal cybersecurity team.
* **Virtual SOC:** A remote team without a permanent physical facility.
* **Co-Managed SOC:** Internal staff working alongside an external Managed Security Service Provider (MSSP).
* **Command SOC:** A master SOC that oversees smaller SOCs, typically in defense or large telcos.

---

### **The Three Pillars: People, Process, and Technology**
A successful SOC requires coordination between:
1. **People:** Highly trained personnel who can adapt to evolving attack types.
2. **Processes:** Standardized actions aligned with security frameworks like NIST or HIPAA.
3. **Technology:** The right tools (SIEM, EDR, etc.) to perform detection and analysis.

---

### **SOC Roles**
* **SOC Analyst (L1, L2, L3):** Classifies alerts, investigates causes, and advises on remediation.
* **Incident Responder:** Responsible for the initial assessment and threat detection during a breach.
* **Threat Hunter:** Proactively seeks out hidden threats that evade traditional security measures.
* **Security Engineer:** Maintains the security infrastructure (SIEM/SOAR).
* **SOC Manager:** Handles operations, budgeting, and staff management.
